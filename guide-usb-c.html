Buying guide

The USB-C cable guide

Most cables are fine. A few will overheat or quietly charge slower than advertised. Three things separate the good ones from the rest.

Wattage: more is not automatically better
  • 20–30W covers most phones and earbud cases.
  • 65–100W Power Delivery handles tablets and 13″/14″ laptops — the sweet spot for a single daily driver.
  • 240W / USB 4 only makes sense for newer MacBooks, iPads with Pro models and some gaming handhelds. If you don't own one of those, skip it.
The E-Marker chip (the part people forget)

Above 3 A / 100W a cable needs an E-Marker chip. It tells the charger how much current is safe. Cheap cables without it either won't fast-charge or can overheat under load. If a “100W” cable costs almost nothing, that's usually where they cut corners.

Length and build
  • Short cables (0.5–1 m) lose less power and run cooler — ideal for desk setups.
  • Nylon-braided jackets last far longer than smooth PVC, especially at the strain-relief end where most cables die.

Data speed is a separate spec from charging. 480 Mbps (USB 2) is perfectly fine if you only use the cable to charge and plug in files occasionally.
