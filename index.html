Buying guide: what actually matters

Three specs decide whether an accessory is good or a waste of money. Short version, no marketing talk.

USB-C cable: watt, chip, length
  • 100W Power Delivery covers phones, tablets and most 13″/14″ laptops.
  • E-Marker chip is mandatory from 5 A / 100 W — without it the cable can overheat.
  • 240W / USB 4 only makes sense for newer MacBooks and iPads.
  • Data speed is separate from charging: 480 Mbps is fine for file-free charging.
  • Short cables (0.5–1 m) lose less power and stay cooler.
Earbuds: fit, codec, battery
  • Bluetooth 5.3+ = stabler connection, lower lag in videos and games.
  • ANC is worth it on trains and in open-plan offices; skip it if you only listen at home.
  • IPX4 or better for sport — sweat is the main reason earbuds die early.
  • Real battery time is always lower than the advertised figure; 6–7 h per charge is good.
  • Fit beats specs: silicone tips that seal the ear canal give more bass than any codec.
Power bank: mAh, output, travel rules
  • 10,000 mAh ≈ 2–3 phone charges, 20,000 mAh also charges a tablet or laptop.
  • Look at output wattage, not capacity: 20W+ is real fast charging, 12W is not.
  • USB-C PD in/out lets you recharge the bank itself with the same cable.
  • Flights: up to 100 Wh in hand luggage — every model listed here qualifies.
  • Usable capacity is roughly 65–70% of the printed mAh figure. That is physics, not a scam.
